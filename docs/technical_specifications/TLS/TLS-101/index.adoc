== Purpose

Documentation of the shared infrastructure that a venue provides to
support racetracks, the components that make up that infrastructure, and
how they interact with the racetracks and the global repository of data.

Define the architectural philosophy of an ODISC system venue.

Describe how one or more racetracks coexist within a shared facility.

Define the responsibilities of the venue independently of the racetracks
it contains.

Explain that the venue provides shared services and infrastructure while
allowing each racetrack to operate independently.

== Scope and Domain

This document covers concerns related to shared resources and services
that span operations of one or more racetracks.

We will look at how we define a venue, the shared infrastructure (used
for multiple tracks) that exist at that venue, any shared services or
displays that may operate at the track, single and multi-racetrack
operations, and certification of venues.

We will not be covering anything related to the actual racetrack system
and below in the chain of operations as these are covered by later TLS-
documents.

== Design Philosophy

When we talk about a venue, we are talking about a physical location
that has one or more racetracks. This may be in your basement, it may be
at a club, or it may be a commercial venue that hosts five tracks to
choose from. They are all venues of different sizes and types.

While a venue can have one or more racetracks, they should always be
able to operate independently. But they can and should share
infrastructure to reduce operational costs.

Venues should also be able to expand incrementally and be able to
support everything from friends gathering on occasion, to weekly club
races, onto international championships.

Beyond that, the system should also support venues that operate on a
pay-to-drive basis and be able to register time spent on track over a
day, compiled over multiple tracks and races.

In short, a venue is where racing occurs. This specification stops short
of the track but includes all the surrounding support systems.

== Venue Architecture

So, what is part of the venue? There are several items that are key to a
venue.

=== Minimum System Items

Racetracks are of course part of the venue. Otherwise, why would you go
there? But let's leave them for later and focus on the pre- and post-
race support systems.

The venue will need internet connectivity, at least to get started. It
will need networking infrastructure for the support systems to talk to
the racetracks and with the global registry.

==== Race Scheduler

The scheduler is a terminal where the race scheduler (individual) enters
drivers and adds them to race on the various tracks at different times.
Drivers may also be added as marshals for races they are not competing
in.

The scheduler can manage multiple racetracks from a single terminal but
can also have multiple terminals connected to it for large venues.

==== Race Scheduling Displays

The scheduling display shows when races are expected to start, when
drivers need to be trackside, and when marshals and other track
officials should report for duty.

==== Race Result Displays

As the day progresses, races are tallied and the results are displayed
for all to see. This can be combined with the race scheduling display to
alternate between the two as well.

=== Optional Items

Beyond the system items listed above, a menu may also have one or more
of the following items to improve the experience of visiting the venue.
Note that none of these are necessarily part of the ODISC ecosystem, but
rather facilities that may be present and tracked in the global
registry.

* Spectator facilities
* Workshop and maintenance areas
* Shared storage

== Shared Services - Race Scheduler

The race scheduler is the brain of the operations. It handles lots of
little jobs that would otherwise bog down the race controller if
accessed in the middle of a race.

Beyond scheduling of races, the race scheduler handles synchronization
with the global registry into the local registry. This may include
pulling down a driver profile for a guest driver that has just arrived
in the middle of a session and has never been at this venue before.

The race scheduler also keeps a local repository of the firmware needed
to run the racetrack. Ie, it has copies of all the firmware that the
track uses, as well as any firmware required for driver controllers or
decoders that have been used at the track. Retrieval on demand ensures
that each component of the system has a valid working firmware.

The scheduler also provides authentication for and time synchronization
for system components. It can also register and log events that occur
and ensure data is properly recorded.

And finally, when the race day is over or ongoing during the day, the
scheduler can push all the changes made during the day to the global
registry.

If sharing of services improves operation of a racetrack without
impacting the race experience negatively, it belongs at the venue level.

== Racetrack Independence

Each racetrack can receive a scheduled race from the scheduler, load
that into the operating profile of all its subsystems, ensure drivers
get connected, and run the race all the way to the finish flag without
additional input from the scheduler. Driver authentication is the
exception to this as they connect their controller.

During operations, the racetrack is an isolated entity that makes all
decisions on its own. Everything, including driver profiles, is already
pre-loaded by the scheduler into the packaged race.

If one racetrack goes offline for whatever reason, that should not
affect the operation of any other racetrack at the venue assuming that
power is still on.

== Scalability

We see venues as expandable entities. They may start out as a one
racetrack club, that grows into a two-racetrack regional venue. With
time they may even grow into a four-racetrack national championship
venue or a larger international competition venue with eight tracks of
varying difficulty.

The key to scalability is that expansion should never require redesign
or replacement of previous implementations.

== Venue Operations

The ODISC ecosystem needs to support venues so that they can support
drivers and spectators. It should be easy to run multiple races at the
same time, all while other racers are getting themselves scheduled into
future heats and performing maintenance on their cars.

The key here is that the ODISC ecosystem is supportive and not hindering
as it assists in the operation and tracking of races.

== Venue Certification

Certification of venues looks not only at the racetracks, but at the
venue as a complete entity. Naturally the racetracks matter, and a venue
cannot be certified at a higher tier than the highest tier racetrack
that it has.

While much of the certification process is self-reporting of
capabilities, national and international certification will most
probably need a manual process to be implemented where drivers that
compete at a certain tier can be certified to validate venues.

== Future Directions

We have lots of ideas on what venues may want to implement within the
ODISC ecosystem. While many of these may not come to fruition, it does
help get the mind thinking about the possibilities as well as ensuring
that we don't restrict ourselves in one way or another that prevents us
from reaching for new ideas.

Examples:

* Central replay services
* Shared media production
* Live streaming

The examples are intended to illustrate flexibility and are not
commitments to future implementation.
