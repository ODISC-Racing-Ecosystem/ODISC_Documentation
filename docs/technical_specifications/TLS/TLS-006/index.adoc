== Introduction

[quote]
____
When to pit is a strategic decision: 

Are you sure now is the right time?
____


The pit lane enables additional race strategies by allowing 
competitions to simulate factors such as fuel consumption 
and tire wear. Drivers must manage vehicle performance and 
decide when a pit stop provides a competitive advantage.

== Philosophy

A pit lane extends racing beyond pure speed by introducing strategic choices.
The design shall support both simple and advanced race formats while maintaining
predictable vehicle movement and fair competition.

== Capabilities

=== Controller / Integration

The pit lane controller manages pit lane operations and communicates with the
Race Management System (xref:../TLS-002/index.adoc#tls-002[TLS-002]). It processes 
Detection Points (xref:../TLS-003/index.adoc#tls-003[TLS-003]) events, manages 
configured pit lane behavior, and coordinates Lane Changers 
(xref:../TLS-005/index.adoc#tls-005[TLS-005]) within the pit lane.

=== Detection Points

Detection Points define the active areas within 
the pit lane where configured pit operations may occur, such as simulated 
refueling and tire changes.

Detection points are also used to control lane changers and pit exits to
prevent collisions.

Advanced detection logic may be used to coordinate pit exit merging and
determine right-of-way when vehicles return to the racing surface.

=== Lane Changers

If pit entry crosses active racing lanes, additional validation is required 
to prevent unsafe crossing conditions.

Additional Lane Changers may be used internally within the pit lane for
automated box selection, ensuring vehicles enter their assigned pit box or
correctly execute a drive-through penalty.

=== Configuration

The Race Management System shall maintain awareness of vehicle location within
the pit lane and process configured pit lane behavior.

Applicable rules may include:
* Speed Limits
* Refueling
* Tire State
* Driver Swaps

== Design

The design of a pit lane impacts how it can be used during races.

=== Simple

In its simplest form, a pit lane is a bypass lane with an entry and exit 
Detection Point, as well as a pair of detection points for where a 
vehicle should stop.

=== Advanced

For a more advanced pit lane, each competing vehicle may have an assigned 
pit box. Each box has a pair of detection points for the simulated 
refueling and tire service operations.

Once a vehicle passes the end fueling detection point, it shall not merge into
the active pit lane or racing surface until the required path is available.

== Start/Finish Line

The pit lane must incorporate a detection point that aligns with the
Start/Finish Line and Race Timing (xref:../TLS-004/index.adoc#tls-004[TLS-004]).

Any detection point used for start/finish timing shall be connected to the
start/finish line controller and not the pit lane controller, ensuring that
lap timing and race progress are registered by the correct system.

== Advanced Features

Additional features that may be considered and added to the specification in the future include:

* Pit Lane Closure
** Adds the ability to close the pit lane for various reasons as determined by racing regulations
* Pit Lane Status Indicators
** Lights (red or green) that indicate whether the pit lane is open or closed
