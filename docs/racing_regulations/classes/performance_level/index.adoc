

[#performance_level]
= Performance Levels

ODISC establishes four distinct Performance Levels to regulate the mechanical 
allowance and tuning scope of a vehicle. These levels dictate exactly which 
components may be adjusted, modified, or replaced, ensuring a predictable 
and balanced competitive environment across different manufacturer types.

[cols="1,1,6",options="header"]
|===

|Level |Name |Core Definition

|Level 1
|Stock
|Strict factory architecture. +
The vehicle must retain its original out-of-the-box components and 
mechanical design.

|Level 2
|Tuned
|Original structural platform with open running gear. +
The factory chassis and body shell must remain intact, but mechanical 
drivetrain components may be upgraded.

|Level 3
|Modified
|Open structural and mechanical platform. +
The factory body shell must retain its original silhouette, but the 
underlying chassis architecture, motor configurations, and guiding systems 
are entirely open to replacement (including 3D-printed or metal aftermarket 
chassis).

|Level 4
|Unlimited
|Open prototype classification. +
Virtually all restrictions on body materials, chassis composition, weight, 
and motor configurations are removed.
|===

== Level 1: Stock

The Stock level focuses on driver skill, basic maintenance, and maximizing 
the performance of a vehicle as delivered by the manufacturer.

=== Technical Allowances

Maintenance::
Periodic replacement of braids, motor leads, and guide blades with identical 
factory or spec replacements is permitted.

Tires::
Original equipment manufacturer (OEM) tires may be glued to the stock rims 
and trued (sanded) to ensure roundness.

Lubrication::
Standard cleaning, oiling of bushings, and greasing of gears is permitted.

Factory Adjustments::
If the manufacturer included built-in tuning mechanisms (such as body float 
screws, adjustable front axle height screws, or interchangeable motor pods), 
these may be adjusted using the original factory parts.

=== Technical Prohibitions

* No aftermarket mechanical parts (motors, axles, gears, or wheels) may be 
  introduced.
* No alterations to the physical structure of the chassis or body (sanding, 
  cutting, or melting) are permitted.

== Level 2: Tuned

The Tuned level preserves the original chassis engineered by the manufacturer 
but permits racers to eliminate factory defects, vibrations, and performance 
bottlenecks by upgrading the spinning and moving components.

=== Technical Allowances

Drivetrain::
Axles, bushings, bearings, crown gears, and pinions may be replaced with 
high-precision aftermarket components.

Wheels and Tires::
Plastic push-fit wheels may be replaced with precision-machined aluminum or 
magnesium wheels secured via grub screws. Tires are open to approved 
aftermarket compounds (e.g., urethane or rubber).

Motors::
Motors may be swapped, provided they adhere to the specific RPM, torque, or 
casing dimensions defined by the class regulations.

Chassis Preparation::
Minor flashing or molding imperfections may be scraped or sanded off the edges 
of the original chassis to allow the body to float freely.

=== Technical Prohibitions

* The original factory chassis must be retained in its entirety. 
* No modification to the motor orientation (e.g., converting an inline 
  chassis to an anglewinder configuration) is permitted unless supported by 
  the factory chassis design.

== Level 3: Modified

The Modified level allows complete re-engineering of the vehicle's mechanical 
platform. This level is specifically designed to allow rigid or low-performance 
factory vehicles to become highly competitive by adapting them to advanced 
racing architectures.

=== Technical Allowances

Chassis Replacement::
The original manufacturer chassis may be completely discarded and replaced with 
an aftermarket alternative. This explicitly legalizes 3D-printed chassis 
(e.g., SLS nylon, carbon-composite), metal plate chassis, or modular 
sub-chassis systems.

Motor Pods::
Mechanical layouts are completely open. Vehicles may utilize floating, 
multi-piece motor pods to tune chassis torsional flex and weight distribution.

Body Alterations::
Minor internal sanding or material removal from the inside of the body shell 
is permitted exclusively to allow clearance for the new chassis, front axles, 
or larger motor configurations.

=== Technical Prohibitions

* The exterior silhouette, scale dimensions, and material of the original 
  factory body shell must remain intact. Lightweight vacuum-formed bodies are 
  prohibited unless explicitly permitted by the vehicle type rules.

== Level 4: Unlimited

The Unlimited level represents the absolute pinnacle of raw slot car 
performance, where regulations are minimized to encourage scratch-building, 
extreme engineering, and experimental designs.

NOTE: Even in the Unlimited level, ODISC recognizes that the driver must remain 
the ultimate decider of race results, rather than the lightest chassis or the 
fastest motor. Vehicle Type rules must balance raw speed with control limits 
to ensure driver skill dictates the outcome on the track.

=== Technical Allowances

Body Shells::
Bodies are entirely open. Lightweight vacuum-formed polycarbonate (lexan) or 
paper-thin composite bodies are permitted.

Chassis Construction::
Custom scratch-built chassis made from brass wire, steel plate, carbon fiber, 
or multi-material 3D prints are permitted.

Motors and Electronics::
Open motor limits, including specialized strap motors, neo-magnet 
configurations, and onboard custom telemetry or capacitor chips, are permitted.

Tires::
Open tire regulations, explicitly permitting silicone, sponge, or 
liquid-treated tires depending on track surface compatibility.
