= Race Scoring

Race scoring defines how individual heat results are converted into race results.

ODISC separates heat scoring from championship scoring. Heat scoring determines the outcome of individual race events, while championship scoring determines standings across multiple events.

== Heat Scoring

Heat scoring is designed to reward competitive racing throughout the entire finishing order.

Every classified finisher receives heat points. This ensures that every position remains valuable and that competitors continue fighting for position throughout the race.

The objectives of heat scoring are:

* Winning a heat should provide a significant advantage.
* Finishing ahead of another competitor should always provide additional value.
* A single poor result should not eliminate a competitor from contention.
* Non-finishing competitors should not receive competitive points.

Competitors that do not receive a classified finish due to a DNF or DNS receive zero points.

== ODISC Weighted Heat Scoring Model

The ODISC Weighted Heat Scoring Model calculates heat points based on:

* The number of competitors in the heat.
* The finishing position achieved.
* A weighting curve that rewards higher finishing positions more strongly.

The maximum points available for the winner are calculated as:

----
Maximum Points = Number of Competitors x 2.5
----

The winner of a heat receives the maximum available points.

The initial scoring curve uses an exponent of 1.5:

----
Raw Points =
Maximum Points x
((Number of Competitors - Finishing Position + 1)
 / Number of Competitors)^1.5
----

The calculated values are adjusted to ensure the ODISC heat scoring requirements are always met:

* The winner receives the maximum available points.
* The last classified finisher receives exactly one point.
* Each finishing position receives at least one more point than the position below it.
* Intermediate positions are adjusted only when required to maintain these requirements.

The final published scoring tables are the authoritative reference for manual scoring.

== Multiple Heat Events

When a race event consists of multiple heats, each heat is scored independently using the ODISC Weighted Heat Scoring Model.

The final race standings are determined by combining the applicable heat results.

For events with multiple qualifying heats, dropped heat rules may be applied to ensure that a single incident does not determine the final result.

== Dropped Heat Results

Dropped heat results allow competitors to recover from unsuccessful heats while still rewarding consistent performance.

The final race standings are calculated using the highest scoring heat results from each competitor.

The following rules apply:

* All completed heats are counted when three or fewer heats are completed.
* When four or more heats are completed, the lowest scoring results are excluded.
* A minimum of three heat results shall count whenever three or more heats are completed.

Excluded heat results remain part of the official race record and may be used for tie resolution.

Example:

A competitor completing six qualifying heats will have their three highest scoring heats counted toward the final race standings. The remaining three results are excluded from the primary score calculation.

== Heat Result Tie Resolution

If two or more competitors have equal final scores after applying dropped heat rules, the following tie-breaking procedures shall be applied in order.

=== Dropped Heat Comparison

Excluded heat results are compared first.

Excluded results are added back one at a time, beginning with the highest remaining excluded score.

The competitor with the higher restored score is ranked ahead.

This continues until the tie is resolved or all excluded results have been considered.

=== Finishing Position Countback

If competitors remain tied after all heat results have been considered, finishing positions are compared.

The competitor with the greater number of higher finishing positions is ranked ahead.

The comparison is performed in the following order:

* Most first-place finishes.
* Most second-place finishes.
* Most third-place finishes.
* Continue through all finishing positions until the tie is resolved.

=== Fastest Lap Time

If competitors remain tied after all heat results and finishing positions have been compared, the fastest valid lap time recorded during the competition shall be used as the final tie-breaker.

The fastest lap time shall be taken from the official race results.

Any lap completed according to the applicable racing regulations may be considered, regardless of race conditions at the time the lap was recorded.

A lap shall not be considered valid for fastest lap purposes if the vehicle deslots and requires reslotting before completing that lap.

A lap affected by a deslot and reslot remains valid for race classification purposes and may contribute toward:

* Completed laps.
* Race distance.
* Finishing position.

The competitor with the fastest valid lap time shall be ranked ahead.

=== Final Tie Resolution

If competitors remain tied after all defined tie-breaking procedures have been applied, the competition regulations shall define the final method of resolution.
