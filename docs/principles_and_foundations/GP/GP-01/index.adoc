== Introduction

The following principles define the intent and vision for racing within the ODISC Racing Ecosystem.

* 01 Better Racing First
* 02 Driver Decisions Matter
* 03 Grassroots Racing First
* 04 Low Barrier to Entry
* 05 Evolution, Not Replacement

== 01 Better Racing First

____
The goal is not to build a better slot car. The goal is to build better
racing.
____

Every engineering, organizational, and architectural decision should
ultimately improve the quality, fairness, safety, accessibility, or
enjoyment of racing.

We want you to remember what happened in a race, on various laps, as
events unfolded. We don't want it to just be a speedy blur that passes
by without being seen.

== 02 Driver Decisions Matter

When every driver's decisions matter, every race tells a story.

The system shall reward driver skill, racecraft, consistency, and
strategic decision-making over hardware advantages or reaction speed
alone.

Learning a track along with how your vehicle performs should matter.
Tracks should require skill to pick out the best racing line, when to
apex, and what lane on the straight provides the best exit three turns
later because of how you are forced into the S-curves and chicanes that
follow the high speed straight. When to stay behind, and when to go
around, these are the decision points drivers will have to make as in
some cases going around means a slower track farther up as the line
through a series of curves takes a much wider path allowing for the
other car to retake that position.

== 03 Grassroots Racing First

Grassroots are the core foundation of the ecosystem. Without them we
cannot grow.

For clarity's sake, we include enthusiast racers with personal tracks at
home where they race against friends in a non-organized group as well as
local clubs in the grassroots category.

Standards shall prioritize practical deployment and operation at the
grassroots level. Capabilities that extend upon the basic and club level
standards are rewarded with higher certification levels, but at no point
shall higher tier standards redefine lower tiers standards in a way that
investments need to be replaced.

Examples: +
The difference between club level and district level certification may
be the number of timing gate sensors and required driver station count.
Club Certification may only need timing sensors before lane changers to
support conflict resolution and broad timing, whereas district level may
need timing gates at a minimum distance of 2 meters apart from each
other. Beyond that, a club certified racetrack may have 4 driver
stations, whereas a district requires 6 driver stations.

By adding additional timing gates, driver stations, and similar
functionality, an existing club certified track can gain district
certification or beyond without having to rebuild the track.

== 04 Low Barrier to Entry

The project shall maintain low barriers to entry and low barriers to
progression.

New racers that want their own gear should be able to be up and running
at their local club or at a friend's track with an off-the-shelf car, a
decoder, and a controller. By shifting everything else to the club
startup costs with shared hardware we reduce the cost to get into the
hobby.

With a small investments clubs and individuals should be able to build
modest track installations that can grow incrementally as their needs
evolve.

By utilizing shared designs with hardware matched to the track we offer
reduced costs for new tracks that can grow and expand over time as the
needs increase.

Our goal is that a club should be able to build a new track consisting
of three 8x4' MDF sheets for the base as well as 3 more for the track, 4
off-the-shelf cars, 4 driver stations, and the electronics to make it
all work for around 1000-1500 Euro / USD (2026 costs). This is a
hardware cost calculation and does not include routing or assembly.

== 05 Evolution, Not Replacement

The project shall preserve compatibility with existing ecosystem
equipment whenever reasonably practical.

Where feasible, existing slot racing equipment, particularly vehicles,
should be reusable through conversion or adaptation. Legacy
infrastructure may be replaced where necessary to achieve the project's
objectives.
